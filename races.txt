Nordic
Hallstatt Nordic 
Based on the anthropological research of Carleton Coon and others, the following quotes and data describe the Hallstatt Nordic type (also referred to as the Osterdal type) in terms of habitat, morphology, stature, and pigmentation.
1. Habitat and Geographic Concentration
•	Modern Center: It is considered the "standard for the type in central and southern Scandinavia today".
•	Specific Concentration: In Norway, it is "especially concentrated in the five valleys of Osterdal, Gudbrandsdal, Valders, Hallingdal, and Numendal".
•	Refuge Area: These valleys are described as a "refuge area of the classic Nordic race" where it exists with "less admixture of other stocks than is the case elsewhere".
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	Height: The type is described simply as "tall". Specifically, men in the Norwegian valleys are cited as "attaining in adult life the mean height of 172 cm".
•	Build: "The bones are typically small and fine, and the general musculature tends to leanness, while corpulence is very rare".
•	Constitutional Type: "A predominantly leptosome constitutional type seems to be characteristic".
•	Limb Proportions: It is characterized as being "relatively long legged and short bodied, moderately broad shouldered, and relatively short armed".
3. Facial Features and Morphology
•	Total Face Shape: The face is described as a "long, narrowish oval, often slightly rhomboid, with prominent bony portions when seen in profile".
•	Nose: The nose is "thin, steep-walled, and high-bridged". In profile, it is "for the most part straight or slightly convex... The tip of the nose is thin... The nasal wings are compressed".
•	Forehead: The forehead is "for the most part sloping" and is "medium to narrow in breadth, and... relatively flat in both planes".
•	Mandible and Chin: It possesses a "long and deep [mandible], curving in front to a well-developed chin, with the gonial angles compressed and usually not visible". There is a "great distance between the borders of the lower teeth and the point of the chin".
•	Vault Shape: The head is a "long oval when seen from above, with almost parallel sides". When seen from behind, it has a "cylindrical transverse profile".
•	Cranial Landmarks: Morphologically, the type has "moderate" browridges, "protruding" occiputs, "flattened" parietals, and "compressed" malars.
•	Cranial Vault: A primary diagnostic is its "notably high cranial vault".
4. Pigmentation
•	Skin: The skin is "almost invariably a pinkish white (von Luschan #3)" and possesses a "fine texture... soft and easily punctured by a hypodermic needle".
•	Hair Color: The "typical hair color... ranges from a medium brown to an ash-blond, with a minimum of rufosity".
•	Hair Form: The hair form is "low-waved to straight".
•	Eye Color: The eyes are "light mixed in typical form, rather than pure light".
•	Eye Morphology: The iris is frequently a "light blue one, with large meshes and iris fibers set quite far apart, so that the iris pattern appears open".

East Nordic
Based on the anthropological research provided in the sources, primarily by Carleton Coon, the following descriptions and data define the East Nordic type (also referred to as the Eastern Nordic or Iranian variety of Nordic).
1. Habitat and Geographic Distribution
•	Geographic Range: The type "inhabited the vast territory from eastern Germany and Austria across the Russian plains to the Altai Mountains in Siberia".
•	Historical Centers: It is identified skeletally among the "Scythians, Sarmatians, and the inhabitants of the Minussinsk kurgans".
•	Modern Survival: Variations of this type are found in the "eastern central Russia" region and the "territory north of the Caucasus".
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	General Stature: The type is described as "tall".
•	Body Build: While specific "East Nordic" body metrics are often grouped with the general Nordic category, they are noted for "tall stature" and "long bones". Among the Scythians, they reached a mean of "over 170 cm".
•	Constitutional Type: They are characterized by a "linear but muscular" build, often heavier than other Mediterranean-derived tall types like the Megalithic.
3. Facial and Cranial Morphology
•	Cranial Vault Height: The primary distinction of the East Nordic is a "low cranial vault". This is frequently contrasted with the "notably high cranial vault" of the Hallstatt (Western) Nordic.
•	Head Form: It is "exceptionally long-headed (dolichocephalic)". Specifically, a mean cranial index of "71" was common among eastern Aurignacian prototypes.
•	Face Shape: It is characterized by a "broader face" than the Western Nordic.
•	Nose Morphology: The type is "leptorrhine (narrow-nosed)". A significant morphological feature is a "greater tendency toward nasal convexity and prominent facial relief".
•	Specific Facial Features: 
o	"High, prominent nose, and wide flaring zygomatic arches".
o	"Compressed zygomata" and "low orbits" are common in the related Corded elements that contributed to the East Nordic.
o	"The malars are large" and there is sometimes a "slight mongoloid suggestion" in certain royal or easternmost variants.
4. Pigmentation
•	General Tendency: The sources state: "There is evidence that the eastern group, at least, was partly if not prevailingly blond".
•	Hair Color: Evidence from plaster death masks in the Minussinsk region (associated with this type) showed "blond hairs pulled out of the beard", while head hair preserved on corpses was "usually brown".
•	Eye Color: In the same eastern regions, eye slits on death masks were "painted blue".
•	Complexion: They are generally described as "light-skinned" or having "white-skinned" characteristics.

Trønder
Based on the anthropological data provided by Carleton Coon and others, the following quotes and metrics describe the Trønder type (also known as the Trøndelagen type), a hybrid Nordic variant characterized by its great size and robustness.
1. Geographical Distribution and Center
•	Primary Center: It is "especially concentrated in South Trøndelag, and most strongly in the valley of Orkdal" in Norway.
•	General Range: This type is "frequent in the central coastal provinces of Norway, north of the Dovre Mountains; the principal form in Iceland, and among the Frisians, and common in the British Isles".
•	Historical and Social Context: In the 12th and 13th centuries, this was "the most populous and most important part of Norway... a center of Norwegian aristocracy".
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	Exceptional Height: The Trøndelagen people are described as "the tallest people in Norway" with the "tallest provincial means".
•	Mean Stature: They are defined as an "extremely tall... type, with a stature of 174 cm".
•	Build: They are "larger-bodied, rugged, and large-boned". The type is "coarsely built" and "heavier in body build... than the Eastern Valley or Hallstatt Iron Age Nordic".
•	Constitutional Type: They are categorized as "large-bodied, rugged, and large-boned, with a great sex difference in stature".
3. Facial and Cranial Morphology
•	Vault Height (Primary Diagnostic): A defining characteristic is the vault height, which is "national high". It attains a mean auricular height of "128–130 mm, which is very great for living races".
•	Head Form: The type is "mesocephalic... [and] higher-headed, longer-faced, less dolichocephalic... than the Eastern Valley or Hallstatt Iron Age Nordic type".
•	Head Shape: The cranium is "better filled, more rounded, and less angular" than the classic Nordic, with a "fuller" temporal region.
•	Face Dimensions: It is characterized by a "long face, narrower in bizygomatic and bigonial widths than [the Borreby]... but wide in both malar and gonial diameters".
•	Facial Relief: They possess "craggier facial features and thicker, coarser soft part anatomy" than other Nordics.
•	Nose: The nose is "high-rooted and high-bridged... typically straight or convex, with many wavy or undulating profile forms".
4. Pigmentation and Pilous System
•	Eye Color: Trøndelagen is cited as the "lightest-eyed region of Norway". There is a "predominance of very light-mixed eyes, especially of the blue variety with a minimum of yellow and brown spotting".
•	Iris Structure: The "typical Trønder iris is close grained and opaque, for the fibers are dense and closely imbricated," appearing less "open" than the eastern Nordic iris.
•	Hair Color: The hair color is characteristically on the golden side, "ranging from brown or golden-brown to golden". Unlike the Eastern Valley Nordics, there is a "low ratio of ash-blond" and a "correspondingly high proportion of golden and brown".
•	Hairiness: The hair is "coarser, and more abundant on beard and body" than the classic Hallstatt type. "Beard and body hair are, as a rule, moderate to abundant".

East Baltic
The East Baltic race is defined as a composite racial type resulting from the blending of unreduced Upper Palaeolithic survivors (specifically the Borreby type) with Corded, Nordic, and Neo-Danubian elements.
1. Geographical Distribution
•	Primary Range: It is "strongly mixed... to form the predominant population of much of Finland and the Baltic States".
•	Wider Influence: The type is found in "northeastern Germany, Poland, the Baltic States, and Finland," and occurs sporadically in Sweden.
•	Eastern Border: It is also prevalent in "western and central Russia [and] northern Poland".
•	Regional Variants: In Finland, anthropologists distinguish between a "western Tavastid subrace" and a "more eastern Savolaxid subrace".
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	General Stature: The East Baltic is characterized by "tall stature".
•	Specific Group Means: 
o	Livs: "Very tall," with a mean stature of 174 cm.
o	Esths: Tall stature of 172 cm or over is typical of the coast, while inland means are around 170 cm.
o	Finns: Tribal differences show means from 165 cm to 172 cm.
•	Build: They are often "large-bodied, larger-headed" and "heavy and powerfully built".
•	Limb and Shoulder Proportions: They are characterized as "large-boned, long-limbed... their shoulders are broad" with a high relative span (long arms) of 107 to 108.
3. Facial and Cranial Morphology
•	Head Form: The race is described as "high-skulled and round-skulled (brachycephalic)".
•	Skull Metrics: For the Livs, the head length mean is 193.3 mm and breadth 155.1 mm, producing a cephalic index of 80.2. The vault height is elevated, with means around 125–127 mm.
•	Total Facial Shape: It features a "broad angular face" and an "elliptical or rectangular" contour.
•	Mandible and Brow: Characterized by an "extremely broad jaw" and a "wide mandible". The bigonial diameter reaches 113–114 mm in Livs and Finns.
•	Nose: The nose is most notably a "stub-nose" or "snub-nosed" form. It is moderately leptorrhine, with a mean index around 63–67. The profile is often "straight... with an upturned snub tip in many instances".
•	Eyes: A distinctive feature is the "wide distance between the eyes".
4. Pigmentation
•	Hair Color: The East Baltic is characteristically "light-blond". In the Livs, the hair is "lighter than that for the kingdom of Norway" and is most frequently "ash-blond or light to medium brown".
•	Eye Color: Eyes are predominantly "pure light or light-mixed". 
o	Livs: 74 per cent possess gray eyes, while blue is exceptional.
o	Latvians: Characterized by "very light, clear-blue eyes".
•	Skin: The skin color is "almost uniformly light" or "fair, but rarely very vascular".

Ladogan
The Ladogan type is defined by anthropologists in the sources as a racial entity originating from the forest-dwelling populations of northern Europe and Russia, representing a survival and reemergence of Upper Palaeolithic hunting and fishing groups that evolved in an incipiently mongoloid direction.
1. Geographical Distribution
•	Primary Region: It is characterized as a "forest-dwelling population of northern Europe east of the Baltic".
•	Russian Forest Zone: The type appeared "sporadically in the forest region of Russia, and occasionally to the south, from the beginning of the Russian Neolithic onward".
•	Specific Populations: It is identified as a primary element among the Ugrian-speakers (such as the Ostiaks and Voguls of the Obi drainage) and the Magyars who migrated from the Volga country into Hungary. It is also found in the Chuvash Republic of eastern Russia.
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	General Stature: The Ladogan is described as having "moderately tall stature".
•	Build: As one of the "unreduced" Palaeolithic survivors, it is characterized by "moderately great head size" and a "wide, heavy mandible," suggesting a robust, heavy-boned frame.
3. Facial Features and Morphology
•	Face Shape: It is distinguished by a "large face" and "wide faces, which as individual examples exceed the accompanying brain case in width". It possesses "laterally prominent malars" (cheekbones).
•	Head Form: The type is described as "mixed meso- and brachycephalic". A prototype is cited as having "hyper-brachycephaly".
•	Nose: Diagnostic features include a "broad, low-bridged nose" with an "elevated snub tip" and "laterally oriented nostril axes".
•	Eyes and Orbits: The type is characterized by "low orbits" and a "wide interorbital distance". A significant morphological trait is the "median eyefold, indicative of both a low orbit and a thick fat deposit in the eye region".
•	General Appearance: It possesses a "vaguely mongoloid appearance" but is also described as having a "Cro-Magnon-like implication" due to its rugged, broad facial structure.
4. Pigmentation
•	Integral Blondism: Unlike some other eastern types, the Ladogan's "tendency to blondism must be considered integral, and not the result of Nordic admixture".
•	Hair and Eyes: Typical descriptions include "blond hair" and "light eyes". Its derivatives, such as the Neo-Danubian, are noted as being "blond or partially blond".
5. Extensive Data Summary
The Ladogan is a composite type resulting from the stabilization of specific ancient blends.
Characteristic	Anthropological Description
Genetic Origin	A blend of a "partly mongoloid brachycephalic element" with a "mesocephalic form of general Upper Palaeolithic aspect".
Head Form	Ranges from mesocephalic to hyper-brachycephalic.
Facial Index	Typically euryene (broad-faced) due to the combination of extreme width and low orbits.
Eye Morphology	Characterized by the median fold and low orbits; the true internal (mongoloid) fold is rare.
Mandible	Notably "wide" and "heavy".
Derivatives	The type serves as the basis for the Neo-Danubian (Ladogan + Danubian) and contributes to the East Baltic (Ladogan + Borreby + Nordic) races.


Slavic
The "Central Slavic" type of Eastern Europe is identified in the sources primarily as the Neo-Danubian race. This type is described as the stabilized result of an ancient blend between the Danubian Mediterranean stock and the Ladogan (incipiently mongoloid) survivors of the northern forests.
1. Geographical Location
•	Primary Distribution: It is "very prevalent among modern Slavs of Poland and Russia, and also among some eastern Germans and Austrians".
•	Regional Concentrations: The type is found "throughout the entire length of the rich agricultural plain which stretches across southern Poland and Russia". In Hungary, it is concentrated in the northeast, "over against Slovakia, and in this section it rises to 60 per cent of the population".
•	Specific Sub-groups: It identifies the majority of the Volhynian Ukrainians and White Russians, and it "forms the common peasant type of eastern Europe".
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	General Height: The type is characterized by "medium stature".
•	National Means: The mean stature for Poles is cited at approximately 165–166 cm, for White Russians at 166 cm, and for Great Russians at 166 cm.
•	Body Build: These populations are often "thick-set to medium" and "long-bodied, with a mean relative sitting height of 53". A common feature is the presence of "broad shoulders and hips".
3. Facial Features and Morphology
•	Total Face Shape: It is frequently described as a "snub-nosed, broad-faced" type. The face is often "round or oval" with "plump, fat-padded cheeks".
•	Head Form: The type is "brachycephalic" or "sub-brachycephalic". In Poland, the index centers between 82 and 83.
•	Nose Morphology: Diagnostic features include a "low nasal skeleton" and a "broad, elevated nasal tip". The profile is often "concave" (25% in Volhynia) or "snubbed".
•	Eye Region: It is characterized by a "median eyefold, indicative of both a low orbit and a thick fat deposit in the eye region". There is also an "exceptional narrowness of the inter-orbital distance" in some Saxon variants, though other Ladogan-influenced types show greater width.
•	Upper Lip: A notable feature is the "long, convex upper lip" and "strong cheek furrows".
4. Pigmentation
•	General Character: The Neo-Danubian is "blond or partially blond" and is described as being "as blond as the Nordic".
•	Specific Combination: The "combination of ash-blond hair with gray-mixed eyes seems to be a Neo-Danubian specialty".
•	Hair Color: In Russia, hair tends toward "brown and ash-blond". In Poland, "truly fair hair is as great here as in Scandinavia".
•	Eye Color: Eyes are predominantly "light-mixed, with gray shades common". In Volhynia, 15% possess pure light eyes.

North Slavic
The "North Slavic" type, primarily identified as the Great Russian population of Northern Russia and the surrounding northeastern European regions, is described by anthropologists as a Slavic base that has absorbed significant Finnic and, to a lesser extent, Scandinavian elements. This type is characterized by its Neo-Danubian and East Baltic racial affinities, distinguished by specific morphological shifts such as nasal flattening and a trend toward brachycephaly.
1. Geographical Distribution
•	Core Area: The type is centered in the "northern districts of the RSFSR," specifically the former "Arkhangelsk, Olonets, St. Petersburg, Novgorod, and Pskov guberniyas."
•	Colonization History: This population is largely regarded as the "descendants of the Novgorod Slavs" (Slovene Novgorodskie) who moved northward into the forest country.
•	Regional Borders: "Northeastern Russia is predominantly East-Baltid and Volgid in race... On the White Sea and around Old Novgorod the Nordid strains increase."
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	Mean Height: The average stature for Great Russians is approximately 166 cm.
•	Regional Variation: Stature varies from "169 cm in the Kuban and Don Cossack country, to about 165 cm in the Finnish territory between the Volga and the Urals."
•	Build: In central Great Russia, the population is described as being "much shorter in stature with coarser and rounder faces" compared to southern Slavic groups.
•	Proportions: Their bodily proportions generally include "a relative sitting height of 53," indicating "long bodies in relationship to leg length," with "broad shoulders and hips."
3. Facial Features and Morphology
•	Nasal Flattening: A primary diagnostic for the Northern Russian group is a "lowering of the nasal bridge and some flattening of the nasal bones."
•	Finnic Substrate: Anthropologists attribute this "flattening of the nasal bones in northern regions" to the "late assimilation... of Finnic ethnic groups, presumably Komi-Zyryans."
•	Face Shape: Faces are "eury- to mesoprosopic," with bizygomatic breadths rarely exceeding 140 mm.
•	Nose Morphology: "Nasal indices usually approach or attain mesorrhiny." There is a "great variability in nasal profile, with at least 25 per cent of concavity in most of the country," including the "snubbed tip so characteristic of northern and eastern Slavs."
•	Head Form: The cephalic index rises from "78 to 79 in parts of the old Scythian country... to 83 and 84 farther north and east."
4. Pigmentation
•	General Character: The type is "predominantly light-mixed," characterized by "brown and ash-blond hair, and light-mixed eyes."
•	Regional Gradient: "Lightest pigmentation is found in the western part of the Great Russian territory, and blondism decreases gradually to the south and east."
•	East Baltic Influence: In the former Baltic lands and Northern Russia, the population is often "very blond," with "ash-blond hair and light blue-grey eyes."
•	Minority Strains: Northeastern Russia also shows "some Nordid and Mongoloid strains," with occasional "Lapp-type types" appearing sporadically.

Southern Slavic(Russia)
In the provided sources, the "South Slavic" type within the East Slavic territory (predominantly the Ukraine and Southern Russia) is characterized by a significant departure from the shorter, more snub-nosed peasant types of the north and center. This southern group is defined by taller stature, a trend toward mesocephaly, and darker pigmentation, often showing influences from ancient Iranian (Scythian) and "Pontic" Mediterranean stocks.
1. Geographical Distribution and Stature
The sources distinguish these populations as the tallest among the East Slavs:
•	Southern Ukraine: "The southeastern Ukrainians, in the country just north of the Black Sea, are tall, with regional stature means as high as 170 cm".
•	Cossack Regions: Great Russians in the south also show increased height, with "regional stature [varying] from 169 cm in the Kuban and Don Cossack country".
•	Siberian Colonists: It is noted that Russians who emigrated to Siberia (often from southern groups) attained a mean of 168 cm, reflecting the genetic potential of these taller southern strains.
2. Head Form and Facial Morphology
Unlike the highly brachycephalic Slavs of Central Europe, the southern East Slavs often retain or re-segregate toward long-headedness:
•	The Mesocephalic Nucleus: A special area in south-central Russia (Tambov, Penza, and Saratov) contains a "mesocephalic nucleus" with a mean cephalic index just under 79 and an average head length of 192 mm.
•	The Pontic/Mediterranean Element: Along the northern shore of the Black Sea, there is a "moderately tall, long-headed, brunet Mediterranean form, which is concentrated along the northern shore of the Black Sea... which the Russian anthropologists call Pontic".
•	Dinaric Influence: In the central and eastern Ukraine, "there is a strong Dinaric element... which often in combination with the Nordic and Neo-Danubian takes on a Noric or approximately similar form".
•	Facial Relief: In contrast to the "flat-faced" northern types, these southern groups show "maximum of facial relief," with malars that are "laterally compressed" and noses that are "thin and often beaked". In the Tambov-Saratov group, the nasal profile is convex in 25 per cent and straight or wavy in 65 per cent.
3. Pigmentation and Pilous System
The southern Slavic regions are notably darker than the northern forest and central plain zones:
•	General Character: "The Ukrainians who live farther east, along the northern shore of the Black Sea, are not only taller... but also darker in hair and eye color".
•	Hair and Eyes: In the south-central Russian mesocephalic group, "head hair is dark brown (Fischer #4-5) in 30 per cent... medium brown (#6-10) in about 50 per cent," with about 8 per cent brown eyes and the rest mixed.
•	Hair Abundance: Unlike the "relatively glabrous" northern Slavs, southern groups often show more abundant beard and body hair, reflecting Mediterranean and Dinaric admixtures.

Alpine
Based on the anthropological research provided in the sources, particularly by Carleton Coon, the following descriptions and data define the core Alpine race. It is characterized as a "reduced and somewhat foetalized survivor of the Upper Palaeolithic population".
1. Geographical Distribution and Habitat
•	Central European Focus: The Alpine race is concentrated in the "central highland and forest zone of Europe and Asia, all the way from the Pyrenees to the Pamirs".
•	Western Nucleus: Its westernmost nucleus is found in "southern France in the Massif Central, which is the home of the Alpine race in its truest form".
•	Other Primary Centers: Significant concentrations are also located in "southern Germany (specifically Bavaria), northern Italy, and southern Albania".
•	Eastern Limits: The type follows the mountain chains into "western and central Asia" and is a principal element among the "Mountain Tajiks from the Pamirs".
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	General Stature: Alpines are described as being of "medium to short stature".
•	Build: They are characteristically "lateral in bodily build" or "thick-set".
•	Constitutional Type: They often exhibit a "pyknic" or stocky habitus, with broad shoulders and a long trunk.
•	Sexual Differentiation: There is a "reduction in skeletal sex differentiation" compared to unreduced Upper Palaeolithic types.
3. Facial Features and Morphology
•	Head Form: The race is defined by a "globular (brachycephalic) head". The cranial vault is typically of "moderate size".
•	Total Face Shape: The face is characteristically "round" or "short and broad".
•	Face Dimensions (Metrical): 
o	Bizygomatic Breadth: Approximately 140 mm.
o	Total Face Height: Approximately 121–122 mm.
o	Forehead and Jaw: Both are "moderately broad," with minimum frontal and bigonial means of about 108 mm.
•	Nose Morphology: The nose is typically "short and straight" or "concave". It is "moderately leptorrhine" with an index of approximately 64–65.
•	Eye and Brow: The orbits are "low" and the browridges are typically "moderate" rather than heavy.
•	Chin: The chin is described as "receding" or of moderate prominence, and the mandible is relatively large compared to Mediterranean types.
4. Pigmentation and Pilous System
•	General Character: Pigmentation ranges from "blond to brunet, but is usually intermediate".
•	Hair Color: The characteristic shade is a "dark to medium brown, which often has a reddish glint".
•	Eye Color: Eyes are typically "brown" or "mixed".
•	Hairiness: Alpines are among the "hairiest-bodied and heaviest-bearded groups of men". The beard is often "heavy" and the head hair is described as "stiff but wavy, unruly".

Dinaric
The Dinaric race (also known as the Adriatic or Dinarid type) is defined in the sources as a primary branch of the Mediterranean stock that has undergone a specific morphological transformation. It is characterized as being "very tall, dark, high-skulled and round-skulled (brachycephalic), with a very large, long, but also rather broad, face and with a large, more or less bent nose".
1. Geographic Distribution
•	Primary Nucleus: The "nucleus of which stretches along a narrow belt from Croatia to the Drin River in Albania, and which reaches its peak in Montenegro".
•	General Range: It forms the "basic population of the whole Dinaric-Alpine highlands from Switzerland to Epirus, also in the Carpathians and Caucasus, as well as Syria and Asia Minor".
•	Expansion: The type is found in the "highland zone of western Asia," where a parallel reemergence of Alpine-Mediterranean hybrids occurs.
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	Exceptional Height: Dinarics are characterized by "tall stature". The Montenegrin nucleus contains the "tallest people in Europe," where men frequently reach means of 177 cm to 178 cm.
•	Build: The type is generally "large-bodied, rugged, and large-boned". It is characterized by a "linear but muscular" build with "long legs and relatively short arms".
•	Extremities: They possess "thin extremities" and "long, thin fingers".
3. Cranial and Facial Morphology
•	Head Form: The Dinaric head is "highly brachycephalic" with a mean index typically around 85 to 87.
•	Occipital Flattening: A diagnostic feature is the "flattening of the occiput" (planoccipitaly). The "foramen magnum and auricular passages are set disproportionately far to the rear," and the head vault is often "national high".
•	Nasal Morphology: The nose is "salient and frequently convex," described as a "large, more or less bent nose". It is "high-rooted and high-bridged" and "extremely narrow" (hyperleptorrhine). The nasal index is often below 60.
•	Facial Structure: The face is "frequently elongated" and often takes the form of an "inverted triangle," where the "forehead is wider than the mandible". It is further marked by "craggier facial features" than the classic Nordic type.
•	Orbits and Brow: The type usually has "high orbits" and "moderate to heavy" browridges.
4. Pigmentation and Pilous System
•	General Tone: Dinarics are typically of "intermediate pigmentation," described as being "rather dark" or "predominantly brunet".
•	Hair and Eyes: The head hair is characteristically "dark brown" or "black". Eye color is prevailingly "brown" or "dark-mixed" (specifically green-brown mixtures), though pure light eyes appear in some northern variants.
•	Beard and Body Hair: The type is notable for "heavy beard and body hair" and thick, often "concurrent eyebrows".
•	Skin: The unexposed skin is generally "brunet-white or light brown".

Mediterranean 
Western Mediterranean
The provided sources, primarily the work of Carleton Coon and Bertil Lundman, describe the Western Mediterranean population of the Iberian Peninsula (Spain and Portugal) as the "classic Mediterranean territory" of Europe. Anthropologically, this region is defined by its overwhelming adherence to several brunet Mediterranean sub-races.
1. Geographic Location and Racial Character
•	Spain's Basic Character: "Despite the complex political history of Spain, the living population is basically and almost wholly Mediterranean".
•	Portugal's Status: "Portugal is, on the whole, fully as Mediterranean in race as is Spain and, perhaps, in some respects, it is more so... it remains, as it has been since the days of the Muge shell-fish eaters, classic Mediterranean territory".
•	Primary Racial Zones: 
o	North Spain: "Predominantly West-Mediterranid in race, with several local types... there are also Nordid strains [and] Alpine strains in the Asturian mountain region".
o	Southern Spain and Portugal: Occupied by "a branch of the East-Mediterranean race—the South-Mediterranean or Saharid subrace".
o	Southeast Coast: Features a "strong Litorid mixture in a wedge-shaped area".
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	General Stature: Regional means in Spain vary from 161 cm to 168 cm. In Portugal, the national mean is 163.5 cm, with provincial means ranging between 162 cm and 165 cm.
•	Bodily Habit: The type is characterized as "slender, lithe, and gracile in body form". 
o	Andalusians: Described as having a "lithe body" with a mean stature of 166.5 cm and a "light mean body weight of 124 lbs".
o	Basques: Distinguished by "their athletic body-build or constitution".
o	Proportions: The Mediterranean groups are "characteristically long-legged and short-bodied" with a mean relative sitting height of approximately 50.6 to 51.3.
3. Cranial and Facial Morphology
•	Head Form: The population is "almost everywhere mesocephalic," though Spain contains regions of relative dolichocephaly. 
o	Portugal: Maintains some of the "lowest cephalic index means on the continent," with a national mean of 76.4.
o	Metrical Means (Madrid Series): Head length 191 mm, breadth 150 mm, and an auricular height of 126 mm.
•	Facial Dimensions: 
o	Narrowness: The bizygomatic diameter is consistently narrow, averaging 132–133 mm.
o	Mandible: Noted for its "shallowness and fragility," with bigonial breadths of about 101–102 mm.
o	Nose: The "upper face height and nose height are great, no matter how small the other dimensions".
•	Morphology: 
o	Nose Shape: Profiles are "straight in 72 per cent," convex in 15 per cent, and concave in 13 per cent.
o	Basque Specialization: Features a "sharp chin and the rather large nose," with an "extremely slender and narrow" mandible.
o	Andalusian Features: Characterized by "flat temples and finely modeled nose and chin".
4. Pigmentation
•	Skin Color: "Divided between a light brown (45 per cent) and brunet-white (45 per cent)" in Portugal. In Andalusia, 80 per cent have "light brown skin" (#15–18 on the von Luschan chart).
•	Hair Color: 
o	Spain: Black hair is found in roughly 29 per cent, dark brown in 68 per cent, and "traces of blondism are visible in 17 per cent".
o	Portugal: "Almost uniformly brunet... dark brown amounts to 68 per cent of the total; blond and red hair is limited to 2 per cent".
•	Eye Color: 
o	Spain: Brown eyes characterize 68 per cent, black 14 per cent, and blue 18 per cent of the population.
o	Portugal: "7 per cent of 'blue,' 15 per cent of 'medium,' and 78 per cent of 'dark'".

The anthropological sources, primarily the work of Carleton Coon, describe the Central Mediterranean racial landscape (predominantly the territory of Italy) as a complex zone where the basic Mediterranean stock has been modified by Alpine, Dinaric, and ancient intrusive elements.
1. Place of Residence and Geographic Distribution
•	Southern Italy and the Islands: These regions are identified as remnants of the "brunet Mediterranean race per se". Southern Italy and Sicily serve as the primary focus for the "small or medium-sized heads" associated with the older Neolithic Mediterranean element.
•	Central and Northern Italy: These areas are described as "more typically Alpine racial territory". The northern part of the peninsula, particularly Piedmont, is noted for intense brachycephaly, forming a continuation of the Alpine concentration found in southwestern France.
•	Regional Gradient: Distribution is marked by a clear gradient; "stature increases as one goes northward from Sicily and the toe of Italy; the cephalic index increases in the same manner, as does blondism".
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	Southern Italians and Sicilians: This population is generally "slightly under medium in stature" with a modern mean of approximately 165 cm.
•	Constitutional Type (South): They are characterized as "thick-set, short-necked, short-fingered, broad-handed, and heavy-torsoed" and often "incline to corpulence in middle age".
•	Northern Italians: Stature is more elevated, with regional means in provinces like Piedmont and Veneto estimated at 168 cm.
•	Bodily Proportions: Southern groups show a relative sitting height of 53.3, indicating a "short-legged, short-armed, and long-bodied condition".
3. Facial Features and Morphology
•	Cranial Vault (South): The head size is relatively large for the stature ("mean head length of 191 mm., and breadth of 151 mm."), exceeding typical Mediterranean diameters.
•	Facial Breadths: The facial structure is broader than classic Mediterranean standards, with a "bizygomatic diameter of 140 mm." and a "bigonial of 108 mm.".
•	Nose Morphology: 
o	In the South: The nose is "moderately long and broad" (nasal index 67) with "convex noses more numerous than concave" and a tip that is "as a rule thicker than the European standard".
o	In the North (Bologna): A distinct type exists that is "tall, slender, brunet, [and] long-faced... with a thin, straight or convex nose, and thin lips".
•	Regional Landmarks: Northern types (Bolognese) are associated with "frequent obliquity of the eye slits... highly arched eyebrows and full malars". In the south, "eyebrow concurrency occurs in 70 per cent of the series" and malars show "moderate forward prominence".
4. Pigmentation
•	General Tone: Italy is predominantly brunet, though blondism increases in the north.
•	Southern Italy and Sicily: 
o	Skin: Over 50% possess "definitely light brown or olive-colored" unexposed skin, while exposed skin "often tans to a distinctive reddish-brown".
o	Hair: Black hair is found in 30%, dark brown in 48%, while 16% have "reddish brown shades" or a reddish glint.
o	Eyes: Pure dark eyes characterize 44%, while 50% have mixed eyes (predominantly green-brown).
•	Northern Italy (Bologna): Pigmentation is lighter: 25% black hair, 60% dark brown, and 27% light or light-mixed eyes.
5. Summary of Differentiated Types

In summary, anthropologists define the Italian Central Mediterranean as a region where the ancient Mediterranean base has been largely absorbed into an Alpine superstructure in the north and a Coarse Mediterranean/Alpine blend in the south, characterized by robust facial features and predominantly dark-mixed pigmentation.
Eastern Mediterranean 
Anthropological sources, primarily those of Carleton Coon, describe the Eastern Mediterranean region (Greece, Crete, and Western Turkey) as a territory characterized by the persistence of the Mediterranean race and its modification through brachycephalization and mixture with Alpine and Dinaric elements.
1. The Greeks (Mainland and Macedonia)
•	Geographic Distribution: The modern Greeks are described as the descendants of varied groups across Hellas, Ionia, Thrace, Byzantium, and the Black Sea coasts.
•	Stature and Body Form: The mean stature for Greeks is "about 167 cm". Their bodily proportions are generally "intermediate; the shoulders are broad, the trunk length moderate... relative sitting height of 52.9".
•	Facial Features and Morphology: 
o	Head: They are mostly "low brachycephals" with a mean index of 81 to 82, though Epirotes reach an extreme of 88. The head length is notably long for brachycephals (189 mm).
o	Face: The face is "mesoprosopic" (mean height 124.4 mm) with a "great breadth of the jaw, as compared with that of the forehead". Diagnostic features include "full, curved temples, full cheeks, a laterally prominent malar region, and strongly everted gonial angles".
o	Nose: The nose is typically "long (58.8 mm) and moderately broad (37 mm)". Profiles are "straight in about 45 per cent... convex in about 30 per cent," with a tip that is "as a rule thick, and elevated more often than it is depressed".
•	Pigmentation: Greeks are "predominantly dark". Over "80 per cent have dark brown hair" and over "65 per cent... have pure brown eyes," mostly dark brown. "Eyebrow concurrency" is found in 75 per cent.
2. The Cretans
•	Stature: Cretans are taller than mainland Greeks, with a mean stature of 169 cm.
•	Morphology: They are "mesocephalic, with a mean cephalic index of 79". The head is large (193 mm long, 149 mm broad).
•	Pigmentation: They are somewhat blonder than mainlanders; "35 per cent have pure brown eyes" and "10 per cent are light brown or blond" hair. A notable trait is that "beards are much lighter... 40 per cent have blond or light brown mustaches".
3. The Osmanli Turks (Western Anatolia)
•	Geographic Distribution: Concentrated in Anatolia, representing a blend of indigenous Cappadocian Mediterraneans with intrusive Turkomans.
•	Stature: The mean stature for Anatolia is 167 cm, ranging from 169 cm at Smyrna to 166 cm in the east. The build is often "thick-set or lateral".
•	Facial Features and Morphology: 
o	Head: They are "only moderately brachycephalic" (mean 84.2), with regional peaks of 86.6 in Kastamuni. Heads are of moderate size (181.6 mm long, 152.6 mm broad).
o	Face: The face is "mesoprosopic" (height 122 mm, bizygomatic 140 mm). "Occipital flattening" is present in 54 per cent.
o	Nose: The nose is "fully Dinaric" in morphology. Profiles are "convex... among 58 per cent".
•	Pigmentation: They are "prevailingly brunet," with "dark brown head hair color, in 90 per cent of cases". Pure dark eyes are found in 40 per cent, with another 40 per cent possessing "dark-mixed eyes".
4. The Pontic Mediterranean Type
•	Distribution: Found along the "northern and western shores of the Black Sea" and extending to the "Black Sea port of Odessa".
•	Characteristics: Described as a "moderately tall, long-headed, brunet Mediterranean form". This type is thought to be the "indigenous Neolithic" element of the region.

Levantine
Based on the provided anthropological sources, specifically the detailed research of Carleton Coon and the racial outlines of Bertil Lundman, the Levantine or Near Eastern Mediterranean type (often identified as Arabid or Mediterranean Proper) is centrally represented by the inhabitants of the Arabian Peninsula. This type is described as the "most central, the most highly evolved, and most charac- teristically Mediterranean" form of the white racial stock.
1. Place of Residence and Habitat
•	Core Area: The "basic Mediterranean territory" is defined as the parts of Arabia lying west and north of the Rubaf el Khali (Empty Quarter) desert.
•	Primary Distribution: It inhabits a vast band from the Atlantic coast of Morocco across North Africa, Egypt, Arabia, and Persia into Afghanistan.
•	Specific Centers: In Arabia, this includes the Nejd, Hasa, the Hejaz, and Asir. The Yemen plateau, isolated by a 10,000-foot escarpment, is noted for preserving this Mediterranean type in its "purest form."
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	General Stature: The type is characterized by moderate or short height. In the Yemen highlands, the mean stature is 164 cm, which is considered typical for the smaller Mediterranean race.
•	Tribal Variations: Nomadic tribes such as the Ruwalla Bedawin have a mean stature of 162 cm, while sedentary groups in the north may reach 170 cm.
•	Build: The body build is predominantly leptosome (lean and spare). They are characterized as "slender, lithe, and gracile in body form."
•	Proportions: They are typically long-legged and short-bodied (relative sitting height mean of 51.3) with narrow shoulders (relative shoulder breadth of 21.5). The hands and feet are notably "small and extremely narrow."
3. Facial Features and Morphology
The facial features are distinguished by "refined" rather than rugged qualities and a lack of massive bony structures.
•	The Nose: The standard Near Eastern/Arabian nose is "high-rooted, high-bridged, and narrow." It is frequently convex in profile (50% in Yemen), beaked, or aquiline, with a thin tip that is usually horizontal or slightly elevated.
•	Facial Dimensions: Faces are characteristically narrow. In Yemen, the bizygomatic diameter is 132 mm, and the bigonial (jaw) breadth is 101 mm, reaching a world extreme in narrowness.
•	Facial Length: While total face height is moderate (121 mm), the upper face height (72 mm) is considered great, indicating a "shallow and fragile" mandible compared to the elongated upper segment.
•	Eyes and Brows: Lundman notes characteristic "almond eyes" and a "'Semitic smile' (conditioned by unusually deep Fossa canina)." Eyebrow concurrency (uniting over the nose) is present in 85% of Yemenis.
•	Head Form: They are consistently "high-skulled and long-skulled (dolichocephalic)." In Yemen, head length is 188 mm, breadth 143 mm, and the cephalic index is 76.
4. Pigmentation and Pilous System
•	Skin Color: The unexposed skin is characteristically a "swarthy white" (von Luschan #10 and #11). Where exposed to the sun, it appears light to medium brown.
•	Hair Color: Head hair is black in 90% of the series and dark brown in the remainder.
•	Eye Color: Irises are predominantly dark brown, though approximately 25% possess mixed eyes (green-brown), which Coon describes as a trace of "incipient blondism" endemic to the Mediterranean stock. Pure blue or gray eyes are virtually non-existent in unmixed groups.
•	Hair Form: Straight hair is rare (4%); the majority have low waves or curly ringlets.
•	Pilosity: The beard is usually slight to moderate and seldom covers the entire face; body hair is also generally sparse.

Irano Afghan
The Irano-Afghan race is described by anthropologists as the principal racial element of the western Asiatic highlands, serving as a primary branch of the Mediterranean stock characterized by extreme elongation of the head and face.
1. Geographical Distribution
•	Core Area: It is the "chief population element in the entire highland territory from the western border of Iran to northern India".
•	General Range: The type forms a "steady band of almost pure dolichocephaly" stretching from "the Atlantic coast of Morocco across North Africa, Egypt, Arabia, and Persia into Afghanistan".
•	Specific Regions: It is the "principal element in the population of Iran, Afghanistan, and the Turkoman country," and it also appears in "Palestine, parts of Arabia, and North Africa".
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	Height: The race is described as being "usually of tall stature". In Iran, stature "varies regionally from about 164 to 169 cm., and thus ranges from medium to moderately tall". Pathans average around 170 cm, while some Afghans are shorter at 163 cm.
•	Build: They possess a "linear but muscular" build. Persians are characterized by a "low" relative sitting height, indicating a "long-legged, short-bodied condition" typical of the Mediterranean group.
•	Turkoman Variant: These groups are noted for being "tall-statured... slender build, [with] thin extremities".
3. Facial Features and Morphology
The Irano-Afghan type is defined primarily by its "extreme vault length, face height, and nose height".
•	The Nose: It is a "hook-nosed type" with "extreme nasal convexity and prominence". The nose is "high-rooted, high-bridged, and narrow," and often reaches an "extreme length of 61 mm" among Pathans.
•	Facial Dimensions: The face is "frequently elongated," taking the form of an "ellipsoid" or "attenuated pentagon". Persians have faces "longer than those found in most parts of Arabia," with "leptoprosopic" facial indices.
•	Cranial Form: They are "high-headed and long-skulled (dolichocephalic)". Head lengths are characteristically great, ranging from 188 to 194 mm, while breadths are narrow, averaging 141 to 145 mm.
•	Vault Height: While some groups have low vaults (121–123 mm), the Turkoman variety is distinguished by an "extremely high head vault," reaching an auricular height of 132 mm.
4. Pigmentation and Pilous System
•	General Character: The type is "predominantly brunet," with skin ranging from "brunet-white to a light brown".
•	Hair and Eyes: Head hair is "almost inevitably black or dark brown". Eyes are usually "pure brown," but a "persistent minority of blondism" (roughly 25%) manifests as mixed eyes (green-brown or gray-brown) and lighter beards.
•	Pilosity: The race is characterized by being "heavy-bearded" and possessing "heavy body hair". Eyebrows are "frequently thick, and... concurrent" (uniting over the nose) in a majority of individuals.

Armenoid
Based on the anthropological sources provided, particularly the detailed work of Carleton Coon and the racial summaries of Bertil Lundman, the Armenoid type (also referred to as the Armenid or Anterior-Asiatic race) is defined as a specialized, brachycephalized branch of the Mediterranean stock.
1. Habitat and Geographic Distribution
•	Primary Center: The type is centered in the "highlands of Asia Minor" and the "mountains of Syria," and it reaches its quintessence in the Armenians.
•	General Range: It forms part of the basic population of the "Dinaric-Alpine highlands from Switzerland to Epirus, also in the Carpathians and Caucasus, as well as Syria and Asia Minor."
•	Peripheral Presence: Enclaves are found in "northern Mesopotamia, especially in Mosul," and in "northern Persia," while Armenoid-like individuals appear sporadically as a result of hybridization in "central and southern Italy."
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	Mean Stature: The Armenoid is characterized as having "medium stature," with a total mean of 166 cm.
•	Regional Variation: Stature varies geographically; eastern Armenians (Van, Erivan) average 169 cm, while western groups (Kaisarie, Marash) are shorter at 164 cm.
•	Build: They are described as "very heavy people," with a mean weight of 160 lbs, possessing a "lateral bodily habitus" that is "predominant in all regional groups."
•	Proportions: The type is "thick-set and short-necked," falling into the general "Alpine category" with a relative sitting height of 53.2.
3. Facial and Cranial Morphology
•	Head Form: The Armenoid head is "highly brachycephalic" and "planoccipital" (flat-backed).
•	Cranial Metrics: The mean cephalic index is 85.4, varying from 84 in the east to 87 in the west. The head length is approximately 185 mm and the breadth 158 mm.
•	The Nose: This is the most diagnostic feature, described as "high rooted, convex, and the tip depressed, especially in advanced age." It is "extremely long" (mean height 60 mm) and "quite wide" (mean breadth 38 mm).
•	Facial Structure: The face is "very large, long, but also rather broad" (total height 128 mm, bizygomatic breadth 144 mm).
•	Specific Features: 
o	"The forehead is as a rule quite sloping."
o	"The mandible is less shallow than with most Mediterraneans" and often shows "flaring gonial angles."
o	It is characterized by an "inverse jaw-forehead ratio" where the jaw is relatively broad compared to the forehead.
4. Pigmentation and Pilous System
•	General Tone: The pigmentation is "almost entirely brunet."
•	Hair and Eyes: "Head hair color ... dark brown (58%) ... black (18%)" and "73 per cent of brown eye color." Pure light eyes are rare, though "mixed and light eyes ... total 34 per cent in Van."
•	Skin: The skin color is mostly "brunet [or] swarthy," described as "dark-skinned" compared to other Caucasian peoples.
•	Pilosity: A standout feature is that the "pilous development of beard and body [is] abundant." They are among the "hairiest-bodied and heaviest-bearded groups of men."
•	Eyebrows: The eyebrows are usually "thick, and in 73 per cent of cases, concurrent" (uniting over the nose).

North Asian
Anthropologists in the sources describe the North Asian racial type—centered in regions such as Yakutia, Buryatia, Tuva, northern Mongolia, and Evenkia—as the most "fully evolved" and "purely mongoloid" branch of the human family. This group is generally subdivided into two primary morphological types: Type A (Tungusic or Taigid) and Type B (Buryat-Mongol or Kumid).
1. The Tungusic Type (Taigid Subrace)
This type is associated with the Tungus-speaking peoples of Evenkia and the Yakuts of Yakutia, as well as Neolithic remains from the Lake Baikal region.
•	Geographic Distribution: Found among the living Tungus and many Siberian peoples, including "Palaeasiatics". It is described as a "historically recent intruder" into the forested northern parts of Asia.
•	Cranial Morphology: This type is characterized as "more long-skulled (dolichocephalic)" or mesocephalic, with an exceptionally "low and slanting" forehead. The cranial vault is among the lowest for mankind, ranging in height from 120 to 130 mm.
•	Facial Features: The face is described as "enormous" and extremely flat, with "extremely large and prominent" malars (cheekbones). A diagnostic feature is that the "canine fossa [is] completely lacking". The nose is "long, narrow, and flat," often failing to project in front of the cheekbones.
•	Stature and Build: While one source characterizes the Taigid subrace as "very short in stature" and "thickset," specific measurements for Tungusic males are cited at 164.4 cm.
2. The Buryat-Mongol Type (Kumid Subrace)
This type represents the Mongol-speaking peoples in their "especial purity," primarily located in Buryatia and northern Mongolia.
•	Geographic Distribution: Centered in Buryatia and Mongolia, it also appears among the Kalmucks of the Volga and the Samoyeds of the Arctic rim.
•	Cranial Morphology: It is "strongly brachycephalic" (round-headed), with a mean cephalic index of approximately 83 to 85. Buryat skulls are noted for being "among the largest in capacity known".
•	Facial Features: The face is "broad and low," with lower orbits than the Tungusic type. While the malars are prominent, they are described as "less pronouncedly mongoloid" than in Type A. The nose is short, and the chin is more prominent than in the Tungusic variety.
•	Stature and Build: Stature is described as "moderately short" or "almost medium tall". Specific means for Buryat males are recorded at 160.9 cm.
3. General Pigmentation and Morphology
The broader "North Asian" or "Altaid" group shares a consistent set of pigmentation and soft-part traits:
•	Pigmentation: The skin is described as "thick and dense," with a "grayish yellow to yellowish brown" or "tawny" color. The hair is "coarse, black, and straight," and the iris is "black" or dark brown.
•	Pilous System: There is a marked "scarcity of beard" and body hair; hair is typically absent from the chest and abdomen in over 90% of adult males.
•	The Eye: A primary diagnostic is the "mongoloid fold" (epicanthus), which is found in its highest frequency among the Buryat-Mongols (83%). The eye slits are characteristically "narrow" and often slanted.

Central Asian
Drawing from the anthropological research of Carleton Stevens Coon, Bertil Lundman, and V.P. Alekseev found in your sources, here are the descriptions of the various populations of Central Asia (encompassing Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kazakhstan, and Kyrgyzstan).
Anthropologists define this region as a complex transition zone where the Mediterranean stock (specifically the Irano-Afghan branch) meets the Alpine race (Tajiks) and various degrees of Mongoloid expansion (Uzbegs, Kirghiz, and Kazakhs).
1. The Tajiks (Tajikistan & Uzbekistan Highlands)
The Tajiks are described as the "linguistically unaltered descendants of the pre-Turkish cultivators" who preserved an ancient "ideally Alpine" type in the isolated mountain valleys.
•	Residence: "In the oases of the Zarafshan Valley, in Ferghana, Samarkand, and Bokhara... [and] in the mountains... reaching over the Pamirs into Afghanistan."
•	Facial Features and Morphology: Their dimensions are "as ideally Alpine as one can find in any unsorted population series."
o	Head Form: Highly brachycephalic with a mean index of 86.0; head length averages 180 mm and breadth 155 mm.
o	Face Dimensions: The face height is 124 mm, with a bizygomatic breadth of 141 mm and a bigonial of 108 mm.
o	Nose: Leptorrhine with an index of 65.0; the nose is typically 55 mm high and 34 mm wide.
•	Pigmentation: While predominantly brunet, "the Tajiks... show 10 per cent of light eyes... [and] beards are occasionally light."
2. The Turkomans (Turkmenistan)
Turkomans represent a specialized "Irano-Afghan" Mediterranean population that has largely remained "non-mongoloid."
•	Stature: They are noted as one of the primary "seats of tall stature" in Asia.
•	Morphology: Defined as a "long-faced, high-headed, hook-nosed type."
•	Metrical Data (Turkoman Variant):
o	Head Form: Dolichocephalic (index 75.0–75.5) with an "extremely high head vault" reaching an auricular height of 132 mm. [Source 1, Summary Table]
o	Face: They possess a "slender build [with] thin extremities" and a very long face (130 mm) and nose (index 59.0–61.0). [Source 1, Summary Table]
3. The Kirghiz and Kazakhs (Kyrgyzstan & Kazakhstan)
Anthropologists describe these groups as a "mixed mongoloid-white type" that formed in Central Asia.
•	Facial Features: The Kirghiz face is a "hybrid one in its differential inheritance of dimensions," receiving its breadth from Mongoloid ancestry and its height from Caucasian ancestry.
o	Face Dimensions: Absolute face height is 133 mm (similar to the Tungus), but the bizygomatic breadth is exceptionally wide, ranging from **148 mm
Anthropologists, particularly Carleton Coon and V.P. Alekseev, describe the Kirghiz and Kazakh populations (often grouped historically as the Kirghiz-Kazak) as a "stable hybrid" of the South Siberian group, resulting from the complex mixing of Central Asian Mongoloids with ancient Europoid populations, specifically those of the Andronovo culture.
1. Geographical Distribution and Stature
•	Location: They are described as a "pastoral nomadic nation par excellence," with a home in the Altai Mountains and the Aralo-Caspian plain.
•	Stature: They are generally of "short stature," with tribal means ranging from 160 cm to 165 cm.
•	Bodily Build: They are characterized by a "long-bodied and short-legged" condition, having a high relative sitting height of 54.7, which is among the highest for any white-related group. Their build is described as "robust" and "well-muscled" with "wide shoulders".
2. Cranial and Facial Morphology
The Kirghiz-Kazak type is defined by a blend of Mongoloid breadth and Europoid height in the face.
•	Head Form: They are "completely brachycephalic," with a mean cephalic index of 85. The head is of "considerable size," with a mean length of 188 mm, breadth of 161 mm, and auricular height of 128 mm.
•	Facial Dimensions: The face is "absolutely large" and "broad," with a height of 125 mm and a bizygomatic breadth ranging from 148 to 153 mm.
•	The Nose: The bridge is "often quite high," and the profile is "convex in 50 per cent of cases". This is described as a "pseudo-Armenoid" nose, where the bridge is high by comparison, but the root is typically low.
•	Distinctive Features: 
o	"Malars protrude prominently forward" in over 80% of individuals.
o	The "epicanthic fold" (Mongoloid fold) is present in 15% of the group.
o	The eye slits are characteristically "narrow" and often "oblique".
3. Pigmentation and Pilous System
•	Skin Color: Rarely "pinkish-white" (<5%); over 60% possess a "yellowish tinge" of varying intensity.
•	Hair and Eyes: 
o	Hair: Characteristically "black" in 50%, with only 4% lighter than dark brown.
o	Eyes: 55% have "pure dark eyes" (mostly dark brown), while only 7% or fewer have "light-mixed or light" eyes.
•	Submerged Blondism: Anthropologists note a "submerged blond strain," evidenced by 14% of beards being lighter than dark brown, a significantly higher ratio than that of head hair.
•	Pilosity: There is a marked "sparsity of beard and body hair". Beards are "absent among 10 per cent" and "scanty among 56 per cent," rarely covering the cheeks profusely.

East Asian
Anthropologists in the sources define the East Asian or Mongoloid family as a "specialized" racial group that evolved in northern and eastern Asia from a rugged, hyperborean ancestral strain. This family is primarily categorized into two sub-types: the Kumid (Buryat-Mongol) and the Taigid (Tungusic).
1. Place of Residence and Geographic Distribution
•	Central Heartland: The "mongoloid family of races" is centered in "eastern Asia" and "northern Asia".
•	Expansion Centers: Their dispersions are traced from the "Altai Mountains and Mongolia as a center".
•	Coastal and Insular Extensions: Archeological evidence identifies types that "invaded Japan during Neolithic times" and others, such as the "Eskimo," that represent further developments of the stock.
•	Western Incursions: Mongoloid strains moved into the "forested northern part of Asia, that is in Siberia," and reached the "Kalmuck steppe" near the Volga river.
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	General Build: Both primary sub-races are characterized as "thickset" and generally of "medium to short stature".
•	Type A (Taigid/Tungusic): This group has a stature mean of "164.4 cm for the males".
•	Type B (Kumid/Buryat-Mongol): This type is slightly shorter, with a male mean of "160.9 cm".
•	Proportions: Their build is marked by a "relative reduction in leg length, with a greater trunk height".
3. Facial Features and Morphology
The sources describe the Mongoloid face as possessing a "slighted facial flatness" and a lack of the "massive bony relief" found in many white groups.
•	Cranial Vault: They are "very low-skulled". The Taigid vault height (120–130 mm) is described as "near the low point for mankind".
•	Malar (Cheekbone) Region: A diagnostic feature is the "extremely large and prominent" malars. In many, "malars protrude prominently forward".
•	The Eye: The most distinct feature is the "mongoloid fold" (epicanthus), which reaches its peak (83%) among the Buryats. Eye slits are characteristically "narrow" and often "oblique".
•	The Nose: The bridge is typically "low". In the Taigid type, the nasal bones are "long, narrow, and flat," often failing to "project in front of the malars".
•	Dental and Mandibular Traits: The dental arch of the palate is "U-shaped" and the "canine fossa [is] completely lacking".
4. Pigmentation and Pilous System
•	Skin Color: Described as "grayish yellow to yellowish brown" or "tawny". The skin is "thick and dense".
•	Hair and Eyes: The hair is "coarse, black, and straight". The iris is almost inevitably "black" or dark brown.
•	Pilosity: There is a marked "scarcity of beard and body hair". Hair is often "absent from the chest and abdomen" in over 90% of adult males.

Malay
Based on the provided anthropological sources, specifically the detailed regional observations of Carleton Coon, descriptions of the Malay type (and the related Indonesian stock) are primarily provided in the context of its influence on maritime populations in the Near East and its role as a "Mongoloid factor" introduced through trade and marriage.
1. Place of Residence and Geographic Origin
•	Southeast Asian Hubs: The sources identify the home of the Malay people in the "Malay States and Indonesia," specifically highlighting centers such as "Singapore, Batavia [Jakarta], and Colombo" from which merchants and missionaries operated.
•	Arabian Periphery: A "Mongoloid factor" was introduced into the Hadhramaut valley by merchants who brought "native wives home from Singapore and Java." Furthermore, a "brachycephalic coastal population" resembling Malays is found in the maritime villages and larger towns of the Yemen coast.
2. Stature and Bodily Form
•	General Stature: The Malay-influenced populations are characterized by a "short stature," with a cited mean of approximately 160 cm.
•	Build: They are associated with a "thick-set" or "lateral" bodily build, which contrasts with the slender, linear (leptosome) build of the Mediterranean plateau populations.
•	Proportions: These groups show a "relative sitting height" that is higher than that of pure Mediterraneans, indicating a shorter-legged, longer-bodied condition.
3. Facial Features and Morphology
The "Malay-like" maritime type is defined by a specific combination of brachycephaly and short facial dimensions:
•	Cranial Vault: They are "brachycephalic," with a mean cephalic index of 84. The head is notably short, with an "extremely short mean glabello-occipital length of 177 mm" and a very low vault height of 122 mm.
•	Face Dimensions: The faces are described as "broader... and very short," with a mean total face height of only 118 mm. [430–431]
•	The Nose: The nasal index is approximately 64, which is described as "less leptorrhine" (wider) than the typical Mediterranean standard.
•	Distinctive Landmarks:
o	"Ears frequently show an extreme slant," which is noted as a characteristic of the coastal brachycephalic type associated with Malay resemblance.
o	They often possess "fuller" faces and a "smoothness of feature" compared to the more rugged bony relief of Western Asiatic types.
4. Pigmentation and Pilous System
•	Skin Color: The skin color is noted to be "darker" than that of typical Mediterranean plateau peoples, often leaning toward a "light brown" or "tawny" shade.
•	Hair Form and Texture: A diagnostic feature of the Malay type is hair that is "coarse and straight," a common Mongoloid trait that distinguishes it from the wavy or curly hair of Mediterraneans and Veddoids.
•	Pilosity: The source notes that while they may have some facial hair, they lack the "massive bony relief" and heavy browridges common in many white groups, often possessing a more infantile or smooth facial plane.

Amerindian
Based on the provided sources, anthropologists describe Amerindians (American Indians or American aborigines) as a specialized group developed from a rugged, non-foetalized ancestral stock that originated in the Old World. The following details regarding their distribution, morphology, and pigmentation are available:
1. Places of Residence and Distribution
•	Origin: The basic genetic stock from which the American Indian developed is described as an "amalgamation" of a large, rugged human stem that "passed over Bering Straits in early post-glacial times, if not earlier".
•	Geographic Types: Anthropologists distinguish several regional groups based on morphological similarities to ancient European types:
o	North American Plains: This group is noted for facial features that resemble the large, rugged Upper Palaeolithic men of Europe.
o	Southernmost South America: Specific mention is made of the Onas and Tehuelche of Tierra del Fuego and southernmost South America.
•	Relationship to Other Groups: On physical grounds, evidence suggests the Eskimo is "really close to the American Indian".
2. Facial Features and Morphology
Amerindians are characterized by a morphology that in many ways parallels the "large, rugged, and relatively un-foetalized" hunters of the Upper Palaeolithic.
•	Cranial Dimensions: Cranial series of Ona skulls from Tierra del Fuego are described as essentially duplicating the dimensions of European Upper Palaeolithic man. This includes:
o	Horizontal circumference, glabello-occipital length, and the sagittal arc from glabella to opisthion.
o	Internal biorbital diameter and bizygomatic diameter (face breadth).
o	Length of the foramen magnum.
•	General Cast: In their "totality of facial features," certain types (specifically those of the North American Plains and southernmost South America) resemble the Upper Palaeolithic people who "may be said to have resembled modern white men" in a morphological sense.
•	Mongoloid Element: The sources categorize the American Indian as containing a "mongoloid element" and place them within the "straight-haired class" alongside Lapps and Mongoloids.
3. Stature and Bodily Form
While the sources focus heavily on European populations, they utilize Amerindian groups as benchmarks for extreme "ruggedness":
•	Tehuelche and Onas: These groups are compared to the tall (approx. 173 cm), long-limbed, and slender Upper Palaeolithic Europeans who possessed "broad shoulders" and "large hands and feet".
•	Relative Size: In statistical studies, Amerindian craniological series are used to determine "inter-group amplitudes" when comparing indigenous peoples of Asia and America.
4. Pigmentation
The sources distinguish Amerindians from the white racial stock by their specific pigmentary tendencies:
•	Lack of Blondism: While the white race developed an endemic (and likely recessive) tendency toward blondism, the sources state that "The mongoloids and American Indians living under parallel conditions [of sub-glacial light intensity] apparently lack the initial mutative tendency necessary for its development."
•	Endemic Brunetness: Unlike the Mediterranean stock, which carries a minor "incipient blondism" (manifesting in mixed eyes or lighter beards), Amerindians are typically characterized within the broader Mongoloid-related categories as possessing "grayish yellow to yellowish brown" or "tawny" skin with "black" or dark irises.
5. Pilous System (Hair and Body Hair)
The pilous system of Amerindians is noted for its variability and its relationship to the Mongoloid family:
•	Hair Texture: While many Mongoloid groups are generalized as having coarse hair, the sources specify that "American Indian hair is very variable in this respect [fineness or coarseness]," suggesting less uniformity in texture than in some other northern Asiatic groups.
