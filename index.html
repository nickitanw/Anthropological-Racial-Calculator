📜 About This Project

This Historical Anthropometric Reconstruction Simulator is an educational tool designed to explore and understand the anthropological classification systems that were developed during the late 19th and early 20th centuries (approximately 1880–1940).

During this period, physical anthropologists attempted to categorize human populations based on measurable physical traits such as cranial measurements, facial proportions, pigmentation, and other morphological features. These classifications, known as "typologies," included categories such as Nordic, Alpine, Mediterranean, and others.

This simulator recreates these historical classification methods for educational purposes only. It allows students, researchers, and history enthusiasts to understand:

  • How physical anthropologists of the era conducted classifications
  • The methodology behind historical anthropometric indices and measurements
  • The limitations and outdated nature of typological thinking
  • The historical context of now-rejected racial typologies

📖 User Guide

Getting Started: Click the "Calculator" button to access the main measurement interface. You will see a series of expandable trait cards, each corresponding to a different anthropometric measurement.

Entering Data: For each trait, select from the available options based on visual assessment or measurement. Some traits require actual measurements (in millimeters) that will be automatically converted to anthropometric indices.

Live Scoring: As you make selections, the simulator calculates compatibility scores for each historical type. The scoring panel on the right shows:

  • Individual Type Scores: How well your selections align with each historical type
  • Score Bars: Visual representation of relative alignment
  • Closest Type: The historical type with the highest alignment score
  • Secondary Components: Secondary types showing moderate alignment
  • Trait Contribution Analysis: Shows which traits contributed most positively and negatively to each historical type
  • Type Presets: Built-in reference profiles allow calibration and testing of historical type models
  • Automatic Index Calculation: Cephalic, Facial, Orbital and Nasal indices are calculated from entered measurements

Generating Reports: Click "Generate Full Report" to create a detailed summary of your selections, measurements, and the resulting type classifications. This report can be printed or saved.

Customization: Use the settings icon (⚙️) to customize the color scheme, and the theme toggle button (🌙/☀️) to switch between light and dark modes. Your preferences will be saved automatically.

🧠 Calculation Methodology

The simulator evaluates each selected trait against a historical anthropological reference matrix. Every option contributes positive or negative scores to one or more historical types.

Traits are not weighted equally. Core morphological traits such as cranial indices, facial proportions, nasal morphology, pigmentation, and orbital measurements carry greater influence than minor features.

  • Anthropometric Indices: Cephalic, Facial, Nasal and Orbital indices are calculated automatically from measurements.
  • Morphological Traits: Face shape, nose profile, forehead morphology, eye morphology, chin structure and other visible features contribute to the final score.
  • Pigmentation Traits: Skin, eye and hair pigmentation are evaluated separately.
  • Weighted Scoring: Each trait belongs to a weighting class (A, B, C, etc.) that determines its influence on the final classification.
  • Positive & Negative Contributions: Traits may increase compatibility with some types while reducing compatibility with others.

Final scores represent morphological similarity to reconstructed historical type models and should not be interpreted as ancestry, ethnicity, genetics, or biological classification.

⚠️ Critical Disclaimers

This simulator does NOT represent:

  • Modern genetics or genomic ancestry analysis
  • Scientific validity of racial categories or typologies
  • Contemporary anthropological consensus or best practices
  • Biological reality or actual human variation
  • Ethnicity prediction or determination
  • Race determination or racial classification
  • A statement about the users or subjects being classified

What this simulator IS:

  • A historical reconstruction of early 20th-century anthropometric classification methods
  • An educational tool for understanding historical science and its limitations
  • A reference for studying the evolution of anthropological thinking
  • A record of outdated and now-rejected typological frameworks

Historical Context: The anthropometric typologies represented here have been thoroughly criticized and rejected by modern science. They were based on limited samples, cultural biases, and fundamentally flawed assumptions about human biological variation. This simulator is designed to help users understand why these frameworks were problematic and how scientific thinking has evolved.

For Educational Use Only: This tool is intended for academic research, historical study, and educational purposes. It should not be used to make claims about any individual or population, and the classifications produced by this simulator have no scientific validity or applicability in modern contexts.
